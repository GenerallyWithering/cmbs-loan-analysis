## 60-second pitch
> I wanted to understand how CMBS loans are underwritten, so I took a real loan from a deal that priced this month: Benchmark 2026-B44. I picked loan 3, a $54 million, ten-year, interest-only loan on a Class A office building next to Capital One Arena in DC.
>
> From the SEC prospectus I rebuilt the key metrics in Excel. I got a 2.43x DSCR, 63% LTV and a 19.3% debt yield, which match the deal's own numbers.
>
> On day one it looks very safe. The interesting part is the tenants. Two tenants pay over 99% of the rent, and almost all of it expires before the loan matures. The big one, the law firm Eversheds Sutherland, pays about $81 a foot when the market is about $60, and their lease ends in 2033.
>
> My stress test showed that if they renew at market rent, coverage drops to about 1.7x. More importantly, at refinance the loan would come up roughly $6 million short. So rates aren't the main risk; the 2033 lease is.
>
> My verdict: I'd be comfortable owning the senior and upper-mezzanine bonds, but cautious lower in the stack, where a 2033 problem would show up first.

**If asked how you built it:** say it plainly. You used the SEC prospectus, built and checked the model with help from an AI tool, and verified every input and ratio yourself. That answer is honest, and it shows you can use the tools.
