# Credit memo — 700 6th Street (Benchmark 2026-B44, Loan 3)

**Source:** Benchmark 2026-B44 Mortgage Trust final prospectus (Form 424B2, filed Nov 10, 2026), Annex A-3 pp. A-3-28 to A-3-34 and Annex A-1 p. A-1-9. All figures are as of the Nov 2026 cut-off date. The calculations are in `model/700_6th_Street_CMBS_Loan_Analysis.xlsx`.

## The loan in two sentences
This is a $54.2M, 10-year, fixed-rate (7.38%), full-term interest-only loan that financed FitzWalter Capital's $79.5M purchase of 700 6th Street NW. That is a 304,947 SF, 12-story Class A office building (2009, LEED Platinum) next to Capital One Arena in DC's East End. The building is 76.1% leased, and two tenants pay 99.4% of the rent: the law firm Eversheds Sutherland (68.5% of rent, lease to Apr 2033) and Monumental Sports & Entertainment (31.0%, lease to Sep 2036, with an option to leave in Sep 2031).

## Stress tests
| Scenario | NOI | DSCR (NCF) | Debt yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| NOI −10% | $9.40M | 2.17x | 17.3% |
| NOI −20% | $8.35M | 1.91x | 15.4% |
| Eversheds renews at the market asking rent ($60.53 vs $81.58/SF today) | $7.43M | 1.69x | 13.7% |
| Monumental uses its 2031 termination option | $5.11M | 1.11x | 9.4% |
| Eversheds leaves and the space stays empty | negative | below 1.0x | n/m |

**Refinance in Oct 2036.** My assumptions for a new lender: 30-year amortization, at least 1.25x DSCR and 10% debt yield, at most 65% LTV, and a 10% exit cap rate.
- At underwritten NOI the loan refinances at a 7%, 8% or 9% rate: the largest new loan is about 1.25x the balloon.
- At −20% NOI it is exactly at the limit.
- If Eversheds renews at market rent, the loan is about $5.9M short.

**Interest rates are not what binds.** The DSCR limit still covers the balloon at rates up to about 14%. The binding limit is value (LTV), so the refinance depends on the building's value and the tenant outcomes, not on the rate.

## Top risks
1. **Tenant concentration and rollover.** Two tenants pay 99.4% of rent, and 99.7% of the rent expires before the loan matures. Eversheds' lease ends in April 2033, 3.5 years before maturity.
2. **Above-market rent.** Eversheds pays $81.58/SF for office space against a $60.53 asking rent in the submarket. Renewing at market alone cuts NOI by about 29%.
3. **A weak office market.** East End vacancy is 22.7%. The building is 23.9% vacant, and its NOI fell 28% from 2023 to the trailing 12 months as tenants left and free rent was given.
4. **Interest-only refinance risk.** With no amortization, the full $54.2M is due in 2036, and repaying it depends on the 2033 renewal and on office values. The appraisal already uses an 11.0% cap rate.

## Mitigants
- **Strong coverage on day one:** 2.43x DSCR, a 19.3% debt yield, and NOI can fall 55% before it stops covering debt service. Leverage is moderate: 63% LTV, and the sponsor put in about 32% of the cost as cash equity.
- **Cash management:**
  - excess cash is swept if DSCR falls below 1.50x;
  - a separate Eversheds trigger includes not renewing by its deadline. It traps cash up to $100/SF, about $14.6M, for re-leasing;
  - a hard lockbox;
  - termination fees go into a reserve.
- **Leasing reserve:** $44,489 a month (about $534K a year).
- **Sticky tenants:** Eversheds has been there since 2010. Monumental grew from 7,988 SF to 85,669 SF and sits next to its own arena.
- **Asset quality:** Class A and LEED Platinum, with direct Metro access.
- **No other debt:** no mezzanine or other additional debt is allowed.

## Verdict
**Comfortable at the senior and upper-mezzanine level of the deal (AAA to A). Cautious at BBB- and below.**
- **Why not lower down:** the day-one numbers are strong, and the loan is 6.7% of the pool. But its risk is binary and comes late: it depends on what Eversheds does in 2033. If Eversheds leaves or renews at market rent, the loan most likely cannot refinance at par in 2036 without new equity. That points to an extension or special servicing, and losses would hit the bottom of the capital stack first.
- **What I would want before going lower:** I'd only take B-piece or BBB- exposure priced for a 2033 rollover scenario, after diligence on Eversheds' plans to renew.
- **Limits of this analysis:** I did not model the deal's bond waterfall or the other loans in the pool, so this view is about this loan only.
